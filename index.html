Diabetic Retinopathy Classification

Completed

National AI Competition 2026 · Top 6 finalist, Computing Track · Mar to Jun 2026

A deep learning system that grades the severity of diabetic retinopathy from retinal images, built by our four-member team, Tony Stork. I worked on dataset cleaning, research, coding, and experiments with MaxViT and ConvNeXt models. Our ensemble reached 80.4% validation accuracy.

Project brief, from our competition poster
Problem
Diabetic retinopathy is a condition where high blood sugar damages the delicate blood vessels in the eye. Its effects range from blurry vision to permanent loss of sight.
Our solution
An AI model that predicts which stage of diabetic retinopathy a person has from an image of their retina. It gives the predicted stage, its confidence as a percentage, and the reasoning behind the decision.
Model
An ensemble: several MaxViT and ConvNeXt models each make a prediction, and a majority vote decides the final answer.
Data preparation
Ben Graham preprocessing and resizing, then augmentation with random rotation, horizontal flips, brightness and contrast changes, and YOCO. A weighted sampler balances the classes during training.
Why an ensemble
It reduces individual model errors and the bias from a severely imbalanced dataset, captures more varied visual features, generalises better, and gives more confident predictions.
Limitations
The model does not know the situation behind a case, such as age, gender, or genetic conditions. It can make mistakes, so it cannot give a final clinical decision.
Responsibilities
Do not overstate how effective the AI is. Train only on data that does not infringe on personal information without permission. Maintain, secure, and update the model regularly.

VentPal

Prototype

Xplore AI Hackathon, Hyperbyte.ai · 2025

A conversational AI concept from a two-day hackathon: an affordable way for young people to talk through their problems, by simulated video or audio call. Gemini writes each reply, ElevenLabs turns it into speech, and an avatar speaks it. I worked on the idea, the feature plan, and presenting it.

Project brief, from our hackathon poster
Problem
Many young people lack the resources to get mental health support. Therapy can be expensive, the wait to see someone is long, and it is hard to find a person they trust. General chatbots are an option, but talking to a cold AI does not feel right.
Objective
Give young people an affordable way to share their problems anytime and anywhere.
Solution
An AI chatbot that can be customised to give each person the most suitable support.
Advantages
An interface that simulates a video call with a real person. Individualised, unbiased responses. It recognises how severe a problem is and connects the user to a real professional or a call for real help.
Our plans
Build it as a web platform and launch it inside Sunway University's network first. Then survey users for feedback and improve it.
Ethical consideration
Users choose whether their conversation is recorded. For those who allow it, the conversations are kept properly protected.
